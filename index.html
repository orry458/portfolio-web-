Motion System • Case Study

AI Workflow Concept for Designers

The Problem:

As a designer, I've noticed a huge bottleneck: bridging the gap between raw creative ideas and technical motion work. I wanted to build a concept for a SaaS tool that acts as a true creative co-pilot, helping designers move effortlessly from a blank page to production-ready animation.

The Solution & The 3-Part Workflow:

  • 01

    Phase 1: Intelligent Brainstorming & Concept Mapping

    What it does: Acts as a creative co-pilot to help designers quickly map out motion themes, moodboards, and narrative arcs before touching any design software.

    Why I included it: Shows that good motion design starts well before keyframing—it starts with structured strategic thinking.

  • 02

    Phase 2: Context-Aware Image Generation

    What it does: Generates style-consistent visual assets and vector layouts tailored for UI animation right inside the workspace.

    Why I included it: Highlights the ability to integrate modern AI generation tools directly into a designer’s asset-creation workflow without losing brand or stylistic consistency.

  • 03

    Phase 3: Automated Code & Expression Generation for After Effects

    What it does: Automatically writes complex expressions and code snippets needed to bring the motion to life in Adobe After Effects, cutting down tedious manual scripting.

    Why I included it: Directly solves a major technical bottleneck for designers who have great visual ideas but get bogged down by complex expression syntax.

The Design & Animation Process

  • Storyboarding & Flow: I started by sketching out some quick wireframes and mapping user flows on paper to figure out how a designer would naturally move from a wild idea all the way to exporting the final piece.
  • UI/UX Design: I designed a sleek, dark-mode interface in Adobe Illustrator, using high-contrast accents (inspired by Gemini's look) to guide the eye naturally through each AI step.
  • Motion & Execution: Brought the UI to life with smooth, purposeful micro-interactions, eased keyframing, and sleek transitions in after effects to demonstrate the exact product experience .

Commercial Motion • Case Study

Red Bull : Minimalistic Commercial

The Concept

Red Bull ads are known for their classic, wobbly hand-drawn cartoon style. I wanted to break away from that and try a fresh, modern approach: a short, punchy motion piece that takes their famous "gives you wiiings" tagline literally, using a beam of heavenly light and a touch of humor.

Process & Breakdown

  • 01

    Asset Creation & Prep

    • Generated the initial can visual using Google Gemini.
    • Brought it into Adobe Illustrator to clean up edges, redraw details, and prep all layers for animation.
  • 02

    Motion & Animation (After Effects)

    • Animated the can dropping in with realistic weight, bounce, and a settling wobble next to the logo.
    • Cut to a dark background with a downward beam of heaven light focusing right on the can.
    • Shot the can upward into the light using steep speed graph acceleration, pulling the vertical tagline up with it.
  • 03

    Sound Design (Premiere Pro)

    • Handled the final audio pass in Adobe Premiere Pro.
    • Synced a heavenly choir sound effect to the exact moment the light appears and the can launches upward.

3D Motion • Case Study

Speed Cube: Native 3D Study

Overview

This was one of my early projects, created to test how far I could push 3D directly inside Adobe After Effects without using external 3D software. Building and manipulating 3D objects natively in After Effects is tricky, so the goal here was simply to learn how native layers, extrusions, and scene lighting interact.

What I Did

  • Built in 3D: Created the Rubik's cube and the orbiting text ring using only native After Effects 3D layers.
  • Layer Twisting: Set up the cube parts to rotate on different axes like a real puzzle cube.
  • Tested Lighting: Added native lights to see how reflections and colored glows hit the moving surfaces.

Brand Identity • Case Study

Krit Media: Dynamic Logo Reveal

The Brief

Dynamic Modern Focused

The Concept

The core identity centers on the orange dot serving as both a spark of focus and the link between "Krit" and "Media". Rather than a static reveal, I wanted a short, punchy ident where a single moving spark of light triggers the entire brand lockup into place.

What I Did

  • The Hook: Started with an orange dot zipping across the dark screen to grab attention right away.
  • Building the Core: Swung the central white bar around and snapped it straight into place to form the shared "i".
  • The Full Reveal: Used the shockwave from that snap to kick out the rest of the letters and finish the lockup.

Proximity Motion • Case Study

Warmth: Proximity Motion

Overview

An early project exploring expression-based automation instead of manual keyframing. I wanted to see how distance and proximity code could drive character behaviors and lighting in a clean grid layout.

What I Did

  • Proximity Expressions: Used proximity code so each character’s expression, smile, and head tilt automatically trigger whenever the glowing warm orb gets close.
  • Dynamic Shadows: Created the cast shadows using Fast Box Blur, rigging the anchor point and stretch direction directly to the light orb’s position.
  • Face Glow: Added an inner glow to each face that intensifies as the warm light approaches to simulate surface bounce.
  • Choreographed Path: Keyframed the light orb to weave smoothly through the rows, testing how multiple neighboring layers react at varying distances.

Kinetic Loop • Case Study

Drain: Kinetic Loop

The Concept

A visual metaphor exploring burnout—how surrounding demands steadily drain our focus and energy until nothing is left. I wanted to translate that feeling into motion: a glowing, vibrant core that gradually loses its color and luminance as orbiting particles pull momentum and life away from it, eventually leaving it hollow and dim.

What I Did

  • Elastic Surface Impact: Animated the orb dropping into a pliable floor plane, creating an elastic rebound to establish initial energy and weight.
  • Energy Drain Cycle: Built a color and luminescence shift where the bright blue core turns dull and neutral, transferring its glow to the surrounding dots as they circle.
  • Choreographed Satellites: Rigged orbiting particles to fan out and snap inward, mimicking external pressures feeding off the main core.
  • Continuous Cycle: Tuned easing curves so the drain and reset flow seamlessly back into the loop.
About Me

A little about myself

My journey into design started back in the 9th grade. Looking back, those early designs weren’t great, but by 10th grade, I had taken on my first real gig—handling social media creatives for my brother’s small business alongside local design projects. I kept freelancing all through design school, building up five solid years of groundwork in layout, typography, and visual communication.

I always wanted things to move, though. I experimented with animation for years on the side, but about two years ago, I committed to it professionally as a full-time freelance motion designer and animator. Today, I focus on clean motion that solves visual problems simply.

Off the clock, I’m pretty disciplined and quietly routine-driven: if I’m not glued to my desk working through keyframes, you’ll usually find me at the gym.
